Simplified proofs of sharp tail bounds and the Erdős matching theorem for large matchings
=======================================================================================

This manuscript presents simplified and expanded proofs based on the
work of Chi, Sun, and Wang. The mathematical results are attributed to
their original authors in the abstract, introduction, theorem statements,
and bibliography.

COMPILATION
-----------
Open main.tex as the main document in Overleaf, or run from this directory:

    latexmk -pdf main.tex

The equivalent sequence is:

    pdflatex main.tex
    bibtex main
    pdflatex main.tex
    pdflatex main.tex

The source uses ordinary TeX Live/MiKTeX packages, including amsmath,
amsthm, mathtools, microtype, parskip, booktabs, enumitem, tikz, hyperref,
cleveref, and aliascnt. The figure is supplied, so Python is not needed
to compile the manuscript. No shell escape is required.

EXACT ALGEBRAIC CHECKS
----------------------
With Python 3 and SymPy installed, run:

    python verification/verify_certificates.py

The script was run successfully with SymPy 1.14.0. It verifies the
polynomial identities and positivity certificates described in the
appendix, using exact integer and rational arithmetic. It regenerates
verification_results.json and the two coefficient CSV files in the
verification directory. See verification/README.txt for the full list
of checks. These checks concern the algebraic certificates; they are
not a formal verification of the entire proof.

To regenerate the illustrative figure, install NumPy and Matplotlib
and run:

    python verification/plot_transition.py

SCOPE AND ATTRIBUTION
--------------------
The main sharp tail theorem and the large-matching theorem are credited
to Chi, Sun, and Wang. Local citations identify the sources of individual
lemmas and distinguish inherited arguments from changes in their proofs
or quantitative forms. The approximate fractional-cover statement is
cited in the form given by Cao, Liu, and Zhang, Lemma 3.2; the earlier
regularity and rounding method of Kühn, Osthus, and Townsend is discussed
separately. The bibliography also records earlier work on the matching
conjecture, the probability connection, and the small-deviation bound.
The manuscript follows the proofs of Chi, Sun, and Wang and expands
their reasoning, equality arguments, and quantitative estimates. The
rewrite does not claim a new main theorem or independent priority.

The manuscript omits author names, affiliations, contact details, and
funding statements. The abstract states that all proofs presented here
were generated by GPT on the basis of the arguments of Chi, Sun, and
Wang, with simplifications and expanded details, and are not independent
proofs of the main results.
